The Written Reference — Filling Out a Volleyball Score Sheet

The full written reference for the trainer above. It follows the NFHS score sheet used by high school volleyball in Pennsylvania and most other states; NCAA sheets use the same core notation with a few differences, called out where they matter.

On this page: What you need Symbol key Before the match Lineups Scoring a rally Worked example Substitutions Time-outs Libero tracker End of set Common mistakes FAQ

What you need before you sit down

  • One score sheet per set. Three for a best-of-three JV match, five for a best-of-five varsity match.
  • At least one libero tracker per match, per team that uses a libero.
  • Two pens — blue or black, plus red. The colors alternate by rotation and are how a referee reads your sheet quickly.
  • A straight edge is optional but makes the dash-bar and boxed points far more legible.
  • Both lineup sheets, handed in by each coach before the set starts.

Arrive about 20 minutes before first serve. Header information, set numbers, and team names can all be filled in before warmups end, which leaves you free to watch the court once the whistle blows.

Ink rule: use blue or black for the first time through the rotation, switch to red when the first server comes up again, and switch back to blue or black for a third turn. Alternating colors is what lets anyone glance at a column and see how many times the lineup has gone around.

Before the match — five setup steps

  1. Circle the set number. One sheet per set, so circle 1 on the first sheet, 2 on the second, and so on.
  2. Circle the level — Varsity, Junior Varsity, or Middle School.
  3. Fill in the header: date, home team, visiting team, site, and scheduled start time.
  4. Put each team on the correct side. The team on the left side of the court goes on the left side of the sheet. Teams switch ends between sets, so this changes every sheet — getting it wrong awards points to the wrong team.
  5. Mark first serve with an X. After the coin toss, X the First Serve box for whichever team serves first. Ask the second referee if you missed the toss.

Entering the lineups

Each coach submits a lineup before the set. Copy it exactly, in serving order, using the Roman numerals I through VI:

  • I is the player who will serve first for that team; the rest follow in rotation order through VI.
  • Write the jersey number against each Roman numeral. Names go in the team roster block, not the rotation grid.
  • List the libero's number in the libero box. A libero is not part of the six-player rotation on the score sheet.
  • Once the first serve is contacted, the lineup is locked. A wrong number on the sheet is a scorekeeping error, not a lineup fault — fix it quietly with the second referee rather than changing what the team actually did.

Scoring a rally, point by point

Volleyball uses rally scoring: every rally produces a point for one team, whether or not that team served. What changes is which marks you make.

When the serving team wins the rally

  1. Write the team's next point number in the current server's row.
  2. Slash that same number in the Running Score column.
  3. The same player serves again — nothing else changes.

When the serving team loses the rally (side out)

  1. Draw the dash-bar (-|) in the server's row. That server's term is over.
  2. Move to the other team's grid. Their next server rotates in.
  3. Write their next point number and box it — [ 4 ] — because it is the first point of a new serving term.
  4. Slash that number in their Running Score column.

Every subsequent point in that same serving term is written normally, unboxed. The boxed number is what lets you reconstruct exactly where each side out happened.

Recording substitutions

  • Serving team: write S then entering player / leaving player, e.g. S 7/3.
  • Receiving team: write Sx then the same pair, e.g. Sx 9/4.
  • Record the sub next to the point number in progress, so the score at which it happened is unambiguous.
  • A player who leaves may re-enter, but only into the same serving-order position, and only for the player who replaced them. Tracking this is the whole reason substitutions live on the sheet.
  • Each team gets a limited number of team substitutions per set. Keep a running count — coaches will ask you how many they have left, and it is your sheet that settles it.
  • A libero replacement is not a substitution and does not count against that limit. See below.

Recording time-outs

  • Serving team calls it: write T in the current server's row.
  • Receiving team calls it: write Tx in the current server's row.
  • Either way, write the score at the moment of the time-out in that team's time-out box — both numbers, their score first.
  • Each team gets two time-outs per set, and they do not carry over between sets.
  • If a coach requests a third, the second referee will deny it; do not record anything.

The libero tracker

The libero is the back-row defensive specialist in the contrasting jersey. Because the libero enters and leaves constantly, those movements are recorded on a separate libero tracker sheet, not in the rotation grid.

  • Record the serving-order position the libero replaces and the number of the player replaced.
  • Libero replacements are unlimited, but two of them must be separated by a completed rally.
  • The libero replaces a back-row player only, and leaves for the same player.
  • Under NFHS rules the libero may serve in one rotation position; when that happens, put a triangle around the Roman numeral on the score sheet. In NCAA men's volleyball the libero may not serve at all.
  • The libero may not complete an attack on a ball entirely above the height of the net, may not block or attempt to block, and may not overhand-set a ball from in front of the attack line for a teammate to attack above net height.

For the full set of restrictions, see the dedicated libero rules explainer.

End-of-set checklist

  1. Circle the winning team's final point number.
  2. Confirm the set was won by at least two points — 25 with a two-point margin in sets 1 through 4, 15 with a two-point margin in a deciding set.
  3. Total each team's points and write them in the set score boxes.
  4. Count and record the substitutions each team used.
  5. Record the ending time of the set.
  6. Have the second referee initial the sheet before the next set starts.
  7. Start a fresh sheet for the next set — and remember the teams have switched sides.

Five mistakes that cost scorekeepers the most

  1. Forgetting to switch sides between sets. Every point after that lands in the wrong column.
  2. Missing the box on a side-out point. Without it, nobody can tell where a serving term began.
  3. Recording a libero replacement as a substitution. It burns a sub the team still has.
  4. Writing the sub before the score. Record the point first, then the sub next to it, so the timing is preserved.
  5. Not alternating ink. A single-color sheet is legal but far slower to audit when a rotation is challenged mid-match.

Frequently asked questions

What does the dash-bar mean on a volleyball score sheet?

The dash-bar -| marks the end of a serving term. It is drawn in the server's row the moment the serving team loses a rally, closing out that player's turn at the service line.

Why is the first point after a side out boxed?

The box identifies the first point of a new serving term. When you read the sheet back, every boxed number is a side out, which makes it possible to reconstruct the flow of the set without the running score.

Do libero replacements count as substitutions?

No. Libero replacements are unlimited and are recorded on a separate libero tracker sheet. They never count against a team's substitution limit for the set.

Can the libero serve?

Under NFHS high school rules the libero may serve in one rotation position, marked with a triangle around the Roman numeral. In NCAA men's volleyball the libero may not serve at all.

Why do scorekeepers switch between blue and red ink?

Ink color tracks how many times a team has been through its rotation. Blue or black for the first pass, red when the first server returns, blue or black again for a third pass.

What do I write when the referee calls a replay?

Write R in the current server's row. No point is awarded and the running score does not change. A caught or dropped first toss is a re-serve instead, marked RS.

How many points does it take to win a volleyball set?

Sets are played to 25 points, win by two. A deciding fifth set (or third set in a best-of-three match) is played to 15, also win by two.
