Boys Volleyball in Chester County, PA

Teams · Season calendar · Tryouts · Schedules & scores

Boys volleyball is one of the fastest-growing high school sports in Chester County, Pennsylvania — and one of the least explained. It is played in the spring, not the fall. Most of the county's league play runs through the Ches-Mont League. And unlike almost any other varsity sport, a large share of the boys on the court picked up the game for the first time at a March tryout. This page is the orientation we wish existed when families ask us how it all works.

It is written by the coaching staff at Downingtown East in Exton, one of the county's boys volleyball programs. We have kept it about Chester County boys volleyball generally rather than about our own team — if you landed here looking for the Cougars, the schedule and scores, roster, and coaching staff are a click away.

When boys volleyball season runs in Chester County

Pennsylvania splits volleyball across two seasons, and this is the single most common point of confusion for families moving into the county: girls volleyball is a fall sport, and boys volleyball is a spring sport. A Chester County boys volleyball season looks like this:

PhaseWhenWhat happens
Tryouts & first practiceEarly MarchPIAA spring sports open. Most schools hold tryouts over the first week.
Regular seasonLate March – MayChes-Mont League matches plus non-league play, usually two to three matches a week.
League standings settleMid-MayDivision races finish; results feed District 1 playoff seeding.
PIAA District 1 playoffsLate MaySeeded brackets by classification, hosted at district sites.
PIAA state tournamentEarly JuneDistrict qualifiers advance to the statewide bracket.

Practically, that means a Chester County student who wants to play boys volleyball needs to be watching for announcements in February, because the window to sign up closes before most people realize the season has started. Physicals and district athletic paperwork are usually due before the first tryout date, not after.

League membership is not the same as fielding a team. Every school above belongs to the Ches-Mont League, but boys volleyball is a growing sport and whether a particular school fields a varsity or JV boys team in a particular spring depends on that year's turnout. Before you plan around a matchup, confirm it on the host school's athletics page or its MaxPreps listing for the current season. Division alignment is also reviewed periodically by the league.

Chester County has schools outside the Ches-Mont League too. Public schools in the county's northern tier compete in the Pioneer Athletic Conference, and the county's independent and parochial schools play in their own leagues rather than in Ches-Mont. Those programs still land in the same PIAA or independent-school postseason structure, and they turn up on Chester County non-league schedules — including ours.

The Ches-Mont League and PIAA District 1

Two different organizations govern a Chester County season, and it helps to keep them straight:

  • The Ches-Mont League sets the regular season. League matches make up the bulk of the schedule, produce the division standings, and decide the league title.
  • PIAA District 1 runs the postseason. Chester County sits in District 1 — the largest of the PIAA's twelve districts, covering the Philadelphia suburbs. District 1 seeds and hosts the playoff bracket, and its qualifiers advance to the PIAA state tournament.

Boys volleyball is classified by school enrollment, with separate brackets for larger and smaller schools. Classification assignments and the number of state-tournament spots allotted to each district are set by the PIAA on a two-year cycle, so a county school's playoff path can look different from one cycle to the next.

Tryouts and how to start playing

Boys volleyball is unusual among varsity sports in Chester County: you do not need club experience to make a team. A meaningful share of every county roster is made up of athletes who had never played a competitive match before March of that year. Height helps, but so does being a basketball player who can jump, a soccer player who can move, or a swimmer who is simply in shape.

What actually matters for a first-time player:

  • Paperwork first. A current PIAA physical and your district's athletic clearance need to be complete before the first tryout, not during it.
  • Show up in February-ready shape. Tryouts are short — often three or four days — and conditioning is the fastest thing to judge.
  • Learn the rotation, not the spike. Coaches can teach an arm swing. Knowing where to stand is what gets a new player onto the JV court. Our rotations explainer covers it in about ten minutes of reading.
  • Ask about JV. Most county programs run a junior varsity team specifically so first-year players get real match reps instead of a season on the bench.

If you are a Downingtown East student, our tryouts open with the PIAA spring season in early March and are open to all DE students — contact the coaching staff and we will make sure you are on the list. If you are at another Chester County school, your athletic director's office is the right first stop.

Finding schedules, scores, and standings

There is no single scoreboard for Chester County boys volleyball, which is why this is the question we get most. In practice, four sources cover almost everything:

  • The school's own athletics site — the authoritative source for start times, site changes, and weather postponements. Many county schools use DigitalSports for this.
  • The Ches-Mont League site — league standings and all-league selections.
  • MaxPreps — the best cross-county view: scores, records, and standings aggregated across PIAA District 1.
  • PIAA District 1 — playoff brackets and seeding, published in late May.

For Downingtown East specifically, our schedule and scores page updates through the season with dates, start times, home/away, and results, and program history archives past seasons record by record.

Going to a Chester County volleyball match

If you have never been, here is what a weeknight looks like. Matches are played in the school gym. Junior varsity usually plays first, then varsity, so a night that starts at 5:30 or 6:00 typically runs to about 8:30. Varsity is best of five sets; JV is commonly best of three.

Sets one through four are played to 25 points, win by two, and a deciding fifth set is played to 15. Every rally produces a point for one team — no one has to be serving to score. Teams switch sides between sets. Admission policies are set district by district; some Chester County schools charge a few dollars at the door for regular-season matches and others do not, while district and state playoff matches do charge.

Several county schools stream home matches on the NFHS Network or Hudl, which is worth checking if travel across the county on a school night is not workable. If you want to actually follow what the person at the scorer's table is doing, our volleyball score sheet guide decodes it symbol by symbol — and if you are the parent who got asked to keep book, it will get you through the night.

Offseason volleyball in Chester County

Because the high school season is only about ten weeks long, most of a Chester County player's development happens between June and February:

  • Clinics. Our staff runs DEVB volleyball clinics for local players in the Exton and Downingtown area.
  • Club volleyball. Boys club programs in the greater Philadelphia and Chester County area run roughly November through spring, and are the main path for players targeting college recruitment.
  • Beach and grass. Two-on-two in the summer is the fastest way to improve ball control, because there is nowhere to hide on a two-person side.
  • Open gyms. Many county high schools run informal offseason open gyms; the athletic office will know the schedule.

Frequently asked questions

When is boys volleyball season in Chester County, PA?

Spring. Tryouts open in early March, the regular season runs from late March into May, PIAA District 1 playoffs are in late May, and the state tournament is in early June. Girls volleyball is played in the fall.

Which Chester County high schools have boys volleyball teams?

Most county league play runs through the Ches-Mont League — Avon Grove, Bayard Rustin, Bishop Shanahan, Coatesville, Downingtown East, Downingtown West, Great Valley, Henderson, Kennett, Oxford, Sun Valley, Unionville, and West Chester East. Twelve of those are in Chester County; Sun Valley is in Delaware County. Whether a specific school fields a boys team in a given spring depends on turnout, so confirm on that school's athletics page.

What league and district is Chester County boys volleyball in?

The Ches-Mont League for the regular season, split into National and American divisions, and PIAA District 1 for the postseason.

Do I need club experience to make a high school team?

No. Boys volleyball rosters across the county regularly carry first-year players, and most programs run a JV team so newer players get match reps while they develop.

How long does a match take?

Varsity, best of five, runs about ninety minutes to two hours. With JV playing first, a full night at a county gym is roughly three hours.

Where do I find scores and standings?

The host school's athletics site for schedules, the Ches-Mont League site for standings, MaxPreps for a cross-district view, and PIAA District 1 for playoff brackets. Downingtown East posts its own schedule and results here.

Is there boys volleyball in Chester County outside the school season?

Yes — clinics, boys club programs that run from roughly November through spring, summer beach and grass play, and school open gyms. Our staff runs DEVB clinics locally.
