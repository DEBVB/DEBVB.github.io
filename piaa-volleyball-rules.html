PIAA Volleyball Rules & Scoring

Seasons · Match format · Scoring · District 1 playoff path

Pennsylvania high school volleyball runs on NFHS playing rules, administered by the PIAA. If you have watched college or international volleyball, most of what you know transfers — but the season calendar, the match format, and a handful of rules are specific to the high school game. This page covers what a parent, new player, or first-time scorekeeper needs before a PIAA match.

Before you rely on a number here: NFHS revises its rulebook annually and the PIAA issues sport-specific bulletins each season, including state adoptions of optional rules. Everything below reflects the general shape of the high school game; confirm specific counts and measurements against the current NFHS volleyball rulebook and the PIAA's published materials for the season you are playing.

Match format

  • Varsity: best of five sets. The first team to win three sets wins the match.
  • Junior varsity: commonly best of three sets.
  • Teams switch sides between sets, which is why a scorekeeper starts a fresh sheet each set with the teams on opposite sides of the page.
  • A separate score sheet is used for every set.

How the scoring works

High school volleyball uses rally scoring: every rally produces a point for one team, regardless of who served.

  • Sets 1 through 4: played to 25 points, and a team must win by at least two.
  • Deciding set (the fifth in a best-of-five, the third in a best-of-three): played to 15 points, also win by two.
  • A team wins the right to serve by winning a rally on the opponent's serve — a side out — and rotates one position clockwise when it does.
  • Points won on your own serve do not rotate the team.

How that translates onto paper — boxed side-out points, the dash-bar, and the running score column — is covered in our volleyball score sheet guide, and the rotation mechanics are in volleyball rotations explained.

Rules that are specific to the high school game

The let serve is legal

A served ball that touches the net and continues into the opponent's court stays in play. This has been the rule at every level for years, but it still surprises spectators who learned the game before the change.

Net height differs by gender

Boys volleyball is played at the men's net height and girls at the women's height — roughly 7 feet 11⅝ inches and 7 feet 4⅛ inches respectively. The difference is large enough that it changes what a blocker can reach, which is why boys and girls stat lines are not directly comparable.

Time-outs

Each team gets two time-outs per set. They do not carry over between sets. A request for a third is denied by the second referee, and nothing is recorded on the score sheet.

Substitutions

Each team gets a fixed number of team substitutions per set. A player who leaves may re-enter, but only into the same serving-order position and only for the player who replaced them. Libero replacements do not count against this limit. The exact number of substitutions permitted has changed in recent NFHS rule cycles — check the current rulebook rather than relying on what a coach remembers from a previous season.

Officials

A match is worked by a first referee (the R1, on the stand), a second referee (the R2, on the floor at the net), a scorer at the table, and line judges. The R2 is the official the scorer works with directly — for substitutions, time-outs, and initialing the sheet at the end of each set.

The libero under NFHS rules

NFHS differs from NCAA men's and FIVB rules in one significant way: the libero may serve, in one rotation position. When that happens, the scorekeeper draws a triangle around the Roman numeral on the score sheet.

Everything else is familiar — no blocking, no attacking a ball entirely above net height, unlimited replacements separated by a completed rally, and a contrasting jersey. The full list is in our libero rules explainer.

Districts and the state tournament

The PIAA divides Pennsylvania into twelve districts. Chester County, including Downingtown East, sits in District 1 — the largest district in the state, covering the Philadelphia suburbs.

The path to a state title:

  1. Regular season — league play plus non-league matches, which determines district playoff seeding.
  2. District 1 tournament — a seeded bracket by classification, played in late May for boys.
  3. PIAA state tournament — district qualifiers advance to the state bracket in early June.

Boys volleyball is classified by school enrollment, with separate brackets for the larger and smaller classifications. Classification assignments and the number of qualifying spots per district are set by the PIAA and change on a two-year cycle, so check the current PIAA bracket release for a given season.

The Ches-Mont League

Downingtown East plays its league schedule in the Ches-Mont League, a conference of public high schools drawn mostly from Chester County. League matches make up the bulk of the regular season; results feed into district playoff seeding along with non-league play. Frequently asked questions

When is boys volleyball season in Pennsylvania?

Spring. Tryouts open in early March, the regular season runs from late March through May, district playoffs are in late May, and the state tournament is in early June. Girls volleyball is a fall sport.

How many sets are in a high school volleyball match?

Varsity is best of five — first to win three sets. JV is commonly best of three.

How many points is a high school volleyball set?

25 points with a two-point margin for sets one through four; 15 with a two-point margin for a deciding set.

Does PIAA use NCAA volleyball rules?

No. PIAA plays under NFHS high school rules plus state adoptions. The libero serving rule is the most visible difference from NCAA men's play.

Is a serve off the net still good?

Yes. The let serve is legal at every level of the modern game.

What district and league is Downingtown East in?

PIAA District 1 for postseason play, and the Ches-Mont League for the regular season.
