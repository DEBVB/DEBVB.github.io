Downingtown East dropped a 3–1 decision on the road at Lansdale Catholic, with the Crusaders taking sets one, three, and four to close it out. The Cougars’ lone set win came in set two — a hard-earned 25–23 answer that showed they could fight back. The effort across all four sets was genuine, and the statistical output speaks to a team that competed the full night.

Parker LaVeyra (#4) was the offensive engine. LaVeyra paced the attack with 15 kills on a .162 hitting percentage — doing the heavy lifting throughout and consistently creating opportunities when the team needed a spark.

Ritvik Adoni (#7) orchestrated the offense with 25 assists, keeping the attack varied and forcing Lansdale Catholic to adjust throughout. Behind the ten-foot line, Anish Yasa (#8) was a stabilizing presence with 12 digs, and Sritan Bhumireddy (#1) contributed 7 kills and 8 digs in a well-rounded performance. The team’s 56-dig total speaks to a back row that competed on every ball.

Two matches in, two competitive results against quality opponents. The record doesn’t reflect the kind of volleyball this team is capable of playing — but the experience from these close matches will add up. The standard is there. The next step is finishing.

DE opened the 2026 season at home against Wilson (Reading) in a five-set battle that showcased both the character and potential of this group. Down 0–2 after the first two sets, the Cougars did something that matters — they didn’t fold. Sets 3 and 4 belonged to Downingtown East, forcing a deciding fifth set and showing real resolve under pressure.

Sets 3 & 4 were outstanding. Winning back-to-back sets against a strong Wilson program after falling behind 0–2 is a significant mental and physical achievement. The Cougars showed they can compete at this level when they’re locked in.

The fifth set didn’t go DE’s way, but this match tells a real story about a team with fight in it. Anish Yasa (#8) was the standout performer, leading the offense with efficiency and contributing in every phase of the game. The front row was active all night, putting up an impressive block total that disrupted Wilson’s rhythm throughout.
